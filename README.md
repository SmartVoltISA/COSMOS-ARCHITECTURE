# COSMOS-ARCHITECTURE

**Systematic research architecture for the Universe.**

COSMOS-ARCHITECTURE is a reproducible research environment for studying the structure, evolution and observable anomalies of cosmic objects.

## Scope

- stars and stellar interiors
- exoplanets and planetary structure
- white dwarfs and neutron stars
- compact and exotic objects
- gravity and relativistic effects
- galaxies and large-scale structure
- cosmology
- observational anomalies
- computational and statistical experiments

## Research principle

**Observation → verification → model → experiment → result → archive → next question.**

Interesting explanations are hypotheses, not facts. Unknown is not true. A proposed experiment is not a performed experiment. Negative results are first-class results.

## Evidence states

`PROPOSED → TESTABLE → TESTED → SUPPORTED / REJECTED / UNRESOLVED`

Every substantive result should preserve source, provenance, assumptions, method, parameters, uncertainty, falsifier, result, verification status and version.

## Initial program

### COSMOS-NS-01 — Compact-Core Search

Search astronomical catalogues for objects whose observed mass, radius, density, dynamics or internal-structure signatures cannot be adequately explained by conventional models and may warrant compact-core hypotheses.

The experiment does **not** assume that neutron-core objects exist. Competing explanations must be tested, including ordinary planetary/stellar models, white dwarfs, neutron stars, black holes, measurement/systematic errors and alternative physical models.

## Architecture

```text
FUNDAMENT/      Principles, methodology, epistemology
OBSERVATIONS/   Observations and source records
MODELS/         Physical and computational models
HYPOTHESES/     Explicit testable hypotheses
EXPERIMENTS/    Reproducible experiments
DATA/           Datasets and provenance
ANALYSIS/       Computation and statistical analysis
RESULTS/        Confirmed, rejected and unresolved results
ARCHIVE/        Frozen history and research records
```

## Research discipline

1. Define the observable before interpreting it.
2. Separate observation, inference, model and hypothesis.
3. Quantify uncertainty and alternative explanations.
4. Define a falsifier before evaluating a hypothesis.
5. Prefer independent measurements and independent verification.
6. Preserve rejected hypotheses and failed experiments.
7. Never silently overwrite historical research states.
